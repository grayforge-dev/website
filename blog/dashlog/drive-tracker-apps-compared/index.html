Drive tracker and drive log apps for iPhone and Android, compared.

DashLog next to nine other ways to keep a record of where and how you drive: GPS loggers that keep everything on the phone, the location history built into Google Maps and Apple Maps, social drive trackers and mileage apps. Short answer

If you want every drive recorded without an account and without your routes going to a server, use a logger that keeps drives on the phone. DashLog does that on iPhone and Android and starts recording by itself when the phone connects to CarPlay or the car's Bluetooth, for free. myTracks (iPhone), Driving Tracker and Geo Tracker (Android) also keep tracks on the device. Google Maps Timeline and Apple Maps Visited Places remember where you've been rather than logging drives. Enroad and Wheelz add leaderboards and accounts, and MileIQ and Driversnote are built for tax and reimbursement mileage logs.

Nothing comes from review sites, user reviews or our own guesses. If a vendor doesn't say whether an account is needed, what's free, or how drives can be exported, the table says not stated.

We picked apps that record drives or routes with the phone's GPS and either keep them on the device or are among the most-rated drive trackers on the stores, plus the location history already built into Google Maps and Apple Maps. We haven't driven the same route with every app, so this page doesn't rank them on accuracy or battery use. Both depend a lot on the phone and its settings, and every app here is free to download or try.

App by app

DashLog

Our app. It's a logbook for car trips, motorcycle rides and bike routes. Set it up once and recording starts when your phone connects to CarPlay or your car's Bluetooth and stops when it disconnects: on iPhone that's a Shortcuts automation the app walks you through, and on Android you pair the car in the app. Each drive saves its route, distance, time, speed, altitude and stats, and you can replay the route with the speed changes along the way. Drives export as GPX or CSV, one at a time or for a whole period, and GPX files from other apps can be imported. On iPhone it can also save each drive's GPX file to a folder you choose when the drive ends.3 There's an Apple Watch app on iPhone and a Wear OS app on Android to start and end a drive from your wrist. It's free, with no account and no subscription. The automatic logging guide shows the setup step by step.

myTracks

A GPS logger for iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch, for any kind of trip rather than driving in particular. It needs no account, shows no ads, and its privacy label says the developer collects no data; tracks stay on the device and sync through iCloud to your other Apple devices, including a Mac. Diary Mode runs in the background and creates one track per day, so you get a full daily record without pressing start, but drives aren't split out on their own. Tracks export as GPX, KML, KMZ or ZIP, and OpenStreetMap maps can be downloaded for offline use. It's free with optional Premium features, and it needs iOS 18.6 or later.

Driving Tracker: GPS Trip Log

An Android drive logger that records GPS along with the phone's motion sensors, so each drive gets G-force readings and harsh braking, acceleration and cornering events as well as route, speed and altitude. It says it needs no account and keeps every drive on the device, with internet used only for maps and place names. You start and stop drives yourself in the free version; automatic trip detection, a heatmap of all drives, GPX export and ZIP backups are part of a monthly or yearly Premium subscription. Its Data safety section lists financial info, app activity and app performance data as collected and shared.

Geo Tracker

A GPS tracker with 10M+ downloads on Google Play, made for outdoor trips and travel as much as driving. You start a recording and it keeps going in the background, with distance, speed, altitude and slope statistics and charts. A navigation mode rotates the map with your direction of travel while you drive. Tracks are stored on the phone and the description says they're not sent to any server; they export as GPX, KML or KMZ. It's free, without ads, and funded by optional donations.

Google Maps Timeline

Already on many phones. Once you turn it on in Google Maps, Timeline saves your visits and routes and shows how you traveled, such as walking, biking, driving or on public transport. Google's help pages say the data is saved on each of your signed-in devices, and you can turn on an encrypted backup to Google's servers, separately for each device, so you can move it to a new phone. You can export it as a JSON file. It's the easiest way to answer “where was I that day?”, but the help pages don't describe per-drive speed or statistics, and it needs a Google account.

Apple Maps Visited Places

Apple's equivalent in the Maps app on iPhone, from iOS 26. It keeps track of places you've been, so you can search them by name, category, address or notes and filter by date or city. Apple says visits are end-to-end encrypted, can't be read by Apple, and appear on all your synced devices. It records places rather than routes or drives, and it isn't available in every country or region.

Enroad – Drive Tracker

With 6.7K ratings on the App Store and 25K reviews on Google Play, it's built around driving stats and competition. It detects drives in the background, makes a share card after each one, and adds leaderboards, clubs and a territory game. Its website says each trip is a file on the phone and the cloud backup is a mirror, and a “Keep Everything on This Device” setting stops backups and leaderboard data from leaving the phone. Recording every drive is free; Pro, at $23.99 a year or $9.99 a month, adds Ride Replay, braking, turn and lane-change stats, and the city, country and worldwide leaderboards. Its App Store privacy label lists identifiers and usage data that may be used to track you across other companies' apps and websites.

Wheelz

A social drive tracker that starts a trip when it detects you moving and ends it when you stop, with a live speed view, a timeline of drives, a garage of your cars and leaderboards. Its privacy policy describes accounts created with Sign in with Apple or Google, and lists saved drives and precise location among the data it keeps, with cloud providers such as Google Firebase. Pro costs $4.99 a week, $9.99 a month or $49.99 a year and unlocks unlimited driving history.

MileIQ

An automatic mileage tracker with over 116,000 App Store ratings. It logs drives in the background so you can swipe each one into business or personal, then produces IRS-compliant reports for deductions and reimbursement. You sign up with an email and password, and drives sit in your MileIQ account, which you can also open on the web. The free plan covers 40 drives a month; Unlimited costs $13.99 a month, or $11.66 a month billed yearly.

Driversnote

A mileage logbook for people who drive for work. Trips start automatically once you reach a certain speed, or when you get in the car if you use its optional iBeacon. Each trip can be marked business, personal, medical or charity, and logbooks export as PDF or Excel. The free plan starts with signing up and covers 15 trips a month; Pro is $11 a month before tax, and a yearly plan bought on the web includes the iBeacon.

Also worth knowing

Open Road: Drive Tracker records every drive, works with CarPlay and fills in a map of every road you've driven, with friends on a live map and convoy voice chat; Pro unlocks the community map, live friends and deeper stats, and drives save and sync.27 It's on iPhone (iOS 18+) and Android.28

For a plain route file, Open GPX Tracker on iPhone is free with no ads or in-app purchases, keeps tracks on the phone and says it collects no data; you start each recording yourself.29 On Android, GPS Logger by BasicAirData is free and open source, records offline without built-in maps, exports KML, GPX or TXT, and declares no data collected.30

Vehicle Trip Logbook Tracker on Android can start, pause and resume trips when a Bluetooth device connects or disconnects, and makes PDF, XLS or CSV mileage reports with a subscription paid every six months.31

Which one to use when

  • You want every drive logged on its own, with nothing to sign up for. DashLog records each drive when the phone connects to CarPlay or the car's Bluetooth, free and without an account. On Android, Driving Tracker can do it with Premium.
  • You want to see where you've been, not drive stats. Google Maps Timeline or Apple Maps Visited Places, already on your phone.
  • You want route files for other apps. DashLog, myTracks, Geo Tracker, Open GPX Tracker and GPS Logger all export GPX. DashLog does it for one drive or a whole month at a time, and on iPhone can save each drive's file to a folder automatically.
  • You ride as well as drive. DashLog records motorcycle rides and bike routes the same way as car trips, and myTracks and Geo Tracker are built for any kind of trip.
  • You want to compare drives with friends. Enroad, Wheelz and Open Road have leaderboards, friends and clubs, and work with accounts.
  • You need a mileage log for taxes or reimbursement. MileIQ and Driversnote classify business and personal trips and produce IRS-compliant reports. That's what they're made for.

Check where your drives go

A location history is about as personal as data gets. Listings are written by the people selling the app, ours included, so a few minutes of checking is worth it:

  1. Watch for a sign-in screen. If an app wants an account before it records, your drives are tied to that account. Its privacy policy should say where they're stored and how to delete them.
  2. Read the privacy section. On the App Store it's “App Privacy”, on Google Play it's “Data safety”. Look for location under “Data Linked to You” or “Data Used to Track You”. Both are filled in by the developer.
  3. Record a short drive in Airplane Mode. On most phones GPS keeps working without a data connection. The map may not load, but if the drive still saves with its route, recording doesn't depend on a server.
  4. Export a drive. A GPX or CSV file is a copy you keep, whatever happens to the app or the company behind it.
Location access

An app that records drives on its own needs background location access (“Always” on iPhone, “Allow all the time” on Android) so it can work while it isn't open. Give that permission only to apps you trust with the result.

Frequently asked questions

Which drive tracker apps work without an account?

Of the apps checked on 26 November 2026, DashLog on iPhone and Android, myTracks on iPhone and Driving Tracker: GPS Trip Log on Android say they need no account and keep your drives on the phone, and Geo Tracker on Android says its tracks are stored locally and never sent to a server. Enroad says it can be used without creating an account, though its leaderboards use one. MileIQ and Driversnote start with signing up, and Wheelz's privacy policy describes accounts created with Sign in with Apple or Google.

Is there a free app that logs every drive automatically?

DashLog is free and records each drive on its own when your phone connects to CarPlay or your car's Bluetooth, after a one-time setup, with no account. Enroad is free to record every drive with automatic start, and charges for Pro features such as Ride Replay and advanced driving stats. MileIQ's free plan covers 40 drives a month and Driversnote's 15 trips a month. Driving Tracker's automatic trip detection is part of its Premium subscription, and myTracks' Diary Mode records one track per day rather than separate drives.

Does Google Maps Timeline record my drives?

Once you turn it on in Google Maps while signed in to a Google account, Timeline saves your visits and routes and shows how you traveled, such as walking, biking, driving or public transport. Google's help pages say the data is saved on each signed-in device, with an optional encrypted backup on Google's servers, and that you can export it as a JSON file. They don't describe speed or per-drive statistics.

Is DashLog free?

Yes. DashLog is currently free to download and use, with no subscription and no account. Automatic recording, route replay, statistics and GPX or CSV export are all part of the free app.

How can I check where a drive tracker stores my trips?

See whether the app asks you to sign in before it records, then read its App Privacy section on the App Store or its Data safety section on Google Play and look for location data linked to you. Recording a short drive in Airplane Mode shows whether recording depends on a server, and exporting a drive as GPX or CSV gives you a copy that stays yours whatever happens to the app.
