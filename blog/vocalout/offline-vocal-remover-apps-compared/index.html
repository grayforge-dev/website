Offline vocal remover apps for iPhone and Android, compared.

VocalOut and five other apps that separate songs on the phone itself, next to the two cloud services they usually get compared with. Uploads, accounts, prices, export limits and offline use, taken from each vendor's own listing.

Short answer

If you want vocals removed without your music leaving the phone, use an app that separates on the device. On iPhone that's VocalOut, Stem Splitter – Vocal Remover, Vocal Remover AI : Split Music, Vocal Remover · Stem Splitter or StemLab; on Android, VocalOut or Stemify. Moises and LALAL.AI do the work on their own servers and need an account. The on-device apps differ mainly in how you pay (subscription, one-time or ads) and in how many parts they split a song into.

Nothing comes from review sites or our own guesses. If a listing doesn't say whether an account is needed or whether the app works offline, the table says not stated.

We haven't tested the other apps' sound quality, so this page doesn't rank them on how clean the separation is. Separation quality depends a lot on the song, and every app here is free to download, so you can hear the difference on your own music.

App by app

VocalOut

Our app. It does one job: an instrumental with the vocals removed, or the vocals on their own. The model runs on the phone, there's no account and no limit on songs, and you can listen to every result in the app for free. Taking a result out of the app (to Files or another app, at the full 32-bit quality) is the paid part: $6.99 once, not a subscription. Songs have to be files already on the phone; VocalOut isn't connected to any music service. The step-by-step guide shows the whole flow.

StemLab

The most stems of the on-device iPhone apps here: a 4-stem or a 6-stem model, with export to WAV, FLAC or M4A. It needs iOS 26.2 or later. The listing says no subscription is required and also offers weekly, monthly and yearly plans next to a $59.99 lifetime purchase, so read the purchase screen to see which one you're choosing.

Stem Splitter – Vocal Remover

Of the other iPhone apps, the only one that spells out its free tier: three separations a month with all four stems and MP3 export. Beyond that it's a subscription. It needs an iPhone 12 or newer.

Vocal Remover AI : Split Music

Like VocalOut, it splits a song into vocals and instrumental rather than separate instruments, and it can save video as well as audio. Its App Privacy label says the developer collects no data (as reported by the developer). There's a lifetime option next to the subscriptions.

Vocal Remover · Stem Splitter

Four stems from the open-source Demucs model, no account, and WAV export. The listing mentions importing from a link but doesn't explain how that works, and its description says there are no subscriptions while the in-app purchases are weekly and yearly Pro plans.

Stemify

The only app here with no paid tier at all: free with ads, four stems, WAV export, no account. Its listing says processing takes about 2 to 8 minutes and recommends a phone with 4 GB of RAM or more. Its Data safety section says the app may share location data with third parties.

Moises

A full practice and production suite rather than just a vocal remover, and it runs almost everywhere: phones, tablets, Mac, Windows, the web and Apple TV. Songs are uploaded to your Moises library, which is stored in the cloud; Moises's pricing page says it will never train on your data. The free tier covers vocals, drums and bass with a limited number of uploads a month.

LALAL.AI

On phones, all separation runs on LALAL.AI's servers and you need an account. The free plan lets you preview results, but downloading the full output needs a paid plan. You can pay by subscription or with one-time minute packs. The desktop app has a local, offline mode for Pro subscribers.

Also worth knowing

Logic Pro for iPad includes Stem Splitter, which separates drums, bass, vocals, guitar, piano and other instruments. It needs an iPad with the A17 Pro chip or later, it isn't on iPhone, and it's sold through Apple Creator Studio at $12.99 a month or $129 a year after a 30-day trial. Apple's pages don't say whether Stem Splitter works offline. djay isolates vocals, drums and instrumentals in real time while you DJ (not with Apple Music or Spotify streams). It's built for performing rather than for saving a backing track, so it isn't in the table.

Where VocalOut is weaker

  • Two outputs only. VocalOut gives you the instrumental or the vocals. If you want drums, bass or piano on their own tracks, StemLab, Stem Splitter, Vocal Remover · Stem Splitter, Stemify, Moises and LALAL.AI all split songs into four or more parts.
  • Exporting costs money. Listening is free, but saving a result outside the app needs the $6.99 Pro purchase. Stemify exports WAV for free (with ads), and Stem Splitter's free tier gives you three separations a month with MP3 export.
  • Phones only. VocalOut is made for iPhone and Android phones. If you'd rather work on a computer or in a browser, Moises and LALAL.AI have desktop and web versions.
  • Speed depends on your phone. The work happens on your device, so an older phone takes longer. A cloud service does the work on its own servers instead.
  • Files only. VocalOut can't pull a song from a streaming service or a link. The song has to be saved on the phone first.

Check an offline claim yourself

Listings are written by the people selling the app, ours included. A two-minute test tells you more:

  1. Turn on Airplane Mode and make sure Wi-Fi is off. Then open the app and separate a short song. If it finishes, the separation ran on your phone.
  2. Watch for a sign-in screen. If an app asks you to sign in before it will process anything, read its privacy policy to see what happens to your files.
  3. Read the privacy section. On the App Store it's “App Privacy”, on Google Play it's “Data safety”. Both are filled in by the developer, so read them together with the airplane-mode test.

Frequently asked questions

Which vocal remover apps work offline?

Of the apps checked on 1 November 2026, VocalOut, Stem Splitter – Vocal Remover, Vocal Remover AI : Split Music and Vocal Remover · Stem Splitter on iPhone, and VocalOut and Stemify on Android, say they separate songs on the device without an internet connection. StemLab says separation runs on the device with nothing uploaded but doesn't use the word offline. The Moises and LALAL.AI phone apps process songs in the cloud.

Do Moises and LALAL.AI upload my song?

LALAL.AI's iOS and Android pages say all separation runs on LALAL.AI's cloud servers and that processing requires an account; its desktop app has a local mode for Pro subscribers. Moises describes uploading songs to your Moises library, which is stored in the cloud.

Is VocalOut free?

VocalOut is free to download, and processing and in-app playback are unlimited and free. Exporting results to Files or other apps needs VocalOut Pro, a one-time $6.99 purchase. There is no subscription and no account.

Can VocalOut split drums, bass and other instruments?

No. VocalOut has two modes: a clean instrumental with the vocals removed, or the isolated vocal track. If you need drums, bass or other stems, apps such as StemLab, Stem Splitter - Vocal Remover, Vocal Remover · Stem Splitter, Stemify, Moises or LALAL.AI split songs into four or more parts.

How can I check whether a vocal remover really works offline?

Turn on Airplane Mode with Wi-Fi off, open the app and separate a short song. If it finishes, the separation ran on your phone. Then read the App Privacy section on the App Store or the Data safety section on Google Play, keeping in mind that both are filled in by the developer.
